**Two rules for anyone editing this file:**

1. **If you change a page, change the copy here first. Then paste it into the code.** The code's copy strings are derived artifacts; this doc is the source.
2. **Never say something here that isn't provably true on the day it publishes.** The single biggest failure mode of v1 copy was claims the product couldn't back up (Broadcasting "confirms receipt with 4 frontier models," for example). Every sentence in v2 must survive a hostile reading from a technical prospect who runs `curl` on us.

- The headline narrative is **"AI visibility analytics for B2B SaaS."** Not "AI crawler infrastructure." Not "llms.txt at the edge." The product is the dashboard that shows you how AI sees you.

- **Auto-Manifest and Parasite Blocklist become free tools**, not reasons to buy.
- **llms.txt delivery is a side utility**, not the headline. Mention it once per page max.

## Part 1 — Voice & positioning playbook

### One-sentence positioning
ReadyForLLM is the AI visibility analytics platform for B2B SaaS teams — we show you how ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini describe your brand, which competitors they cite instead of you, and which AI answers are actually driving pipeline to your site.

### One-paragraph positioning
Classic analytics tools tell you which humans visit your site. ReadyForLLM tells you which AI answers send humans to your site — and which ones send them to your competitors instead. Every day we run your most important buyer prompts across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini, extract who gets cited in what order, and correlate that with real referrer traffic from a lightweight tracker on your site. For the first time, a B2B SaaS team can answer "is ChatGPT sending us revenue?" with a number instead of a guess.

### The voice in one line
Calm, technical, honest — like a senior engineer giving you a heads-up over coffee. Not a salesperson pitching you at a conference.

### Always do
- **Lead with the buyer's reality, not our feature.** "ChatGPT mentions 3 of your competitors before you" beats "our dashboard surfaces answer-share metrics."
- **Use specific, provable numbers.** Not "hundreds of crawlers" — "GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Meta-ExternalAgent, Google-Extended, CCBot." Names build trust.
- **Admit what's unknown.** When an LLM provider doesn't publicly confirm behavior, say so. "No AI provider publishes a content-push endpoint, so we don't pretend to notify them" is better copy than a false claim.
- **Write like the reader is smarter than you.** The target reader knows what a CDN is, what a referrer header is, what a zero-click answer is. Don't explain LLMs to them.
- **Use contractions and em dashes.** Formal voice reads as marketing; natural voice reads as human.
- **End on a real ask or a real question.** "Want the full report?" beats "Ready to transform your AI strategy?"

### Never do
- **Never say ReadyForLLM "optimizes" your content for AI.** We measure it. Optimization is what the customer does with what we measure.
- **Never imply we talk to LLM providers on your behalf.** We don't. No provider has a public ingestion endpoint. We measure output; we don't whisper in their ear.
- **Never use "revolutionary," "game-changing," "cutting-edge," "leverage," "synergies," "unlock," or "supercharge."** If a VC deck-generator could write it, we don't ship it.
- **Never pitch llms.txt as a proven SEO win.** It isn't. Major LLMs haven't publicly confirmed consumption. It's a future-proof bet, not a present-tense promise.
- **Never promise "3-minute setup" without the honest asterisk.** Say "install the tracker in 3 minutes, see first data tomorrow" — because the first prompt run takes a Sentinel cycle.
- **Never use emoji in cold outbound or in-app alerts.** The Reykjavík 🇮🇸 sign-off on founder-authored posts is the one exception.
- **No exclamation points anywhere.** Not in headlines, not in CTAs, not in emails.

### The honest value prop — memorize this
> ReadyForLLM gives you three things: (1) daily tracking of how ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini describe your brand across the prompts your buyers actually run; (2) side-by-side comparison with every competitor you care about, so you can see exactly which queries they win; and (3) referrer-level attribution from a one-line tracker on your site, so you know which AI answers drove real traffic and real trials. Works on any host — Vercel, Cloudflare, Netlify, Next.js, WordPress, Apache, Nginx.

### The one-sentence version
> See exactly how AI describes your brand, who it cites instead of you, and which answers are sending real pipeline — updated daily, across every major model.

### The hard truth we lead with
The category is new. No LLM provider publishes guaranteed citation signals. We can't promise your brand will rank #1 in ChatGPT next week. What we **can** do is tell you exactly where you stand today, where your competitors stand, and what's moving — so when you ship a change, you can measure whether it worked. That's the job. Anything more is vendor lies.

A few weeks ago I sat down and did something I should have done three months earlier: I steelmanned the case for not building ReadyForLLM as I'd been describing it.

## Part 13 — The four rules for everything

Any copy that ships under ReadyForLLM must pass these four tests before it goes live:

1. **Could a hostile prospect disprove this with curl?** If yes, rewrite it. The Broadcasting failure was a curl-test failure waiting to happen. Never again.
2. **Does it describe what the product does, or what the buyer gets?** If the first word is "we" or "our," rewrite. The reader wants to read about themselves.
3. **Would a senior engineer find a word that's pretending?** "Leverage," "supercharge," "unlock," "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "industry-leading," "game-changing" — all auto-fail. Delete and rewrite.
4. **Is there a specific number, name, or verifiable fact somewhere in the paragraph?** If not, the copy is slurry. Add one specific thing — a bot name, a model name, a number, a timeframe. Specificity is credibility.

If the copy passes all four, it ships. If it fails one, it gets rewritten before it goes anywhere near a page.
